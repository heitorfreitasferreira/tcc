# Held & Karp (1970) — The Traveling-Salesman Problem and Minimum Spanning Trees

**Reference:** Michael Held, Richard M. Karp, *Operations Research* 18(6):1138–1162, 1970.  
**DOI:** [10.1287/opre.18.6.1138](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.18.6.1138)

---

## 1. Problem and motivation

The symmetric traveling salesman problem (TSP) seeks a minimum-weight Hamiltonian cycle in a complete graph. The authors observe that a variant of the minimum spanning tree — the *1-tree* (a tree on vertices {2,…,n} plus two edges incident to vertex 1) — is a relaxation of a tour: every tour is a 1-tree, and a 1-tree with all vertex degrees equal to 2 is exactly a tour. This connection is exploited to derive computable lower bounds on the optimal tour cost and to design exact and heuristic optimization algorithms.

## 2. Core method or approach

- **1-tree relaxation:** A 1-tree consists of a spanning tree on vertices {2,…,n} plus two edges incident to vertex 1. Minimum 1-trees are computable in O(n²) time via a minimum spanning tree algorithm.
- **Lagrangean dual via edge-weight perturbation:** The transformation cᵢⱼ → cᵢⱼ + πᵢ + πⱼ leaves every tour cost shifted by a constant (2 Σ πᵢ), so TSP is invariant. However, the minimum 1-tree *does* change, allowing maximization of the lower bound w(π) = min cost of a 1-tree under transformed weights.
- **Linear programming equivalence:** Maximizing w(π) is equivalent to solving the LP relaxation of the classic TSP integer program (Program 3). The duality gap f(π) — the difference between optimal tour cost and the best 1-tree bound — is zero iff the LP has an integer optimum.
- **Three solution methods:** (a) Column-generation simplex method where entering variables are generated by minimum 1-tree computations; (b) an ascent method that iteratively finds ascent directions of the concave piecewise-linear function w(π); (c) a branch-and-bound procedure embedding the ascent method, using "out-of-kilter" vertex detection for branching decisions.
- **Generalization via matroids:** The matroid of 1-trees (bases = 1-trees, circuits = 3 edges at vertex 1 or cycles avoiding vertex 1) unifies the theory. Extensions are sketched for the asymmetric TSP (1-arborescences), the *p*-salesman problem (*p*-trees), and single-facility scheduling.

## 3. Main results

- **Bound quality:** max_π w(π) equals the optimal LP relaxation value. A 6-vertex counterexample shows the gap can be strictly positive (tour cost = 4 vs. bound = 3), proving the LP does not always solve TSP exactly.
- **Column-generation performance:** Solves most problems with n = 12 and some with 13 ≤ n ≤ 20; convergence degrades rapidly for larger instances. Observed that many xᵢⱼ variables tend toward 0 or 1 during iterations, suggesting a heuristic rounding strategy that showed promise up to n = 48 (not systematically tested).
- **Ascent method:** Tested on n ≤ 25. Iterations are cheap but number of iterations grows quickly with n. Convergence to a maximum of w(π) was not guaranteed theoretically.
- **Branch-and-bound:** Demonstrated by hand on a 6-city instance, finding an optimal tour of weight 207. The method is guaranteed to terminate with an optimal tour, relying on the monotonicity of bounds and finite branching.
- **Note added in proof:** A subsequent highly effective algorithm for computing max w(π) with improved branching strategy was reported to yield excellent results (details deferred to a sequel).

## 4. Strengths and limitations

**Strengths:**
- Establishes a clean, computationally tractable connection between TSP and minimum spanning trees.
- The Lagrangean dual (maximizing w(π)) provides the best-possible lower bound from the 1-tree relaxation.
- Theoretical framework is mathematically rigorous (linear programming duality, matroid theorems, the greedy algorithm) and generalizes to multiple combinatorial optimization problems.
- The 1-tree lower bound (now called the *Held-Karp bound*) became a cornerstone of modern exact TSP solvers such as Concorde.

**Limitations:**
- Column-generation exhibited slow convergence for n > 12; the ascent method lacked a guaranteed convergence proof.
- Branch-and-bound was tested only via hand computation; no large-scale computational validation was performed.
- The gap f(π) is generally non-zero, so the bound alone is insufficient to solve TSP; explicit enumeration (branching) remains necessary.
- Edge-weight transformation with non-integer π vectors introduces fractional arithmetic, which can degrade numerical stability and convergence speed.

## 5. Practical takeaway for researchers

The Held-Karp 1-tree lower bound is a foundational technique for TSP and related routing problems. Its computational efficiency and strong bound quality make it a standard component in state-of-the-art exact solvers. The paper also demonstrates a broader design principle: relax a hard combinatorial problem into an efficiently solvable subproblem (spanning tree), then use Lagrangean multipliers (π) to tighten the bound, and finally embed the bound in a branch-and-bound search when the gap remains. This template — Lagrangean relaxation + subgradient/ascent optimization + branch-and-bound — has been successfully replicated across many domains of discrete optimization.
